Why we exist

Cybersecurity has a fear problem. We're the alternative.

The industry sells dread: hooded hackers, red padlocks, products to make it stop. We think security is more like gardening — a practice you learn by doing, in good company, season after season.

01 · Locally grown

We meet in person.

Libraries, farmers markets, kitchen tables across Central Illinois. Advice from a neighbor you'll see again next month beats advice from a vendor every time.

02 · Open by default

We run in public.

Our charter, governance, membership roll, and even this website's source live in a public Git repository. Everything we make is CC0 — fork us, remix us, plant your own chapter.

03 · Mutual aid threat model

Nobody secures themselves alone.

We protect each other: the retiree's inbox, the small shop's register, the kid's first Linux install. Security is a shared responsibility, not a subscription.

04 · Playful practice

Humor is part of our threat model.

People learn what they enjoy. We tinker, reverse-engineer, remix, and run tabletop drills with dice. Playfulness is a path to insight — it says so in our charter.

Why the orange? Gardeners plant marigolds among the vegetables because they repel pests. Around here, marigold means a protective action — it's the firewall of the garden.
What we grow

Four programs, all free, all open.

GRC/PROG-01

Patch Parties

Monthly bring-your-device tune-ups. Updates installed, passwords managed, routers tamed, backups started — together, over coffee. Leave with everything patched and a checklist for home.

Now sprouting
GRC/PROG-02

The Seed Vault

Our open knowledge base, plus offline USB knowledge kits you can use when the internet can't be trusted — or isn't there. Preparedness for the digital layer of life.

In the greenhouse
GRC/PROG-03

Gremlin Drills

Lightweight tabletop exercises for families, clubs, and small shops. "Grandma's account just emailed everyone a weird link — what do you do?" Practice the bad day before it happens.

In the greenhouse
GRC/PROG-04

Field Notes

Plain-language dispatches on staying safe online, published to the open web, Nostr, and the Fediverse. No fear-mongering, no affiliate links, ever.

Plant yourself

Four ways to sign. Pick the one that fits your life.

Signing the Statement of Organization makes you a member. It costs nothing, it's voluntary, and you can leave anytime. Whoever shows up is the right person.

🌱 The easy way — two clicks

Type your name here and we'll open your signature on GitHub with everything already filled in — name, type, today's date. You just confirm and submit.

Needs a free GitHub account. No account? Sign on paper instead, or open the blank form.

⚡ The Nostr way — verifiable forever

Type your name in the form to the left, then sign with your NIP-07 extension (Alby, nos2x…). We verify the Schnorr signature before you're added — and anyone can re-verify it, forever. PGP signers: open a PR per the membership spec.

🖊️ The paper way

Sign a printed copy at any Patch Party or community event, witnessed in person. We'll add you to the public roll for you.

📚 The archival way

Upload a public statement of support to the Internet Archive referencing our Statement of Organization. Belt, suspenders, and a time capsule.
