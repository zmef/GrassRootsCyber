# Grass Roots Cyber — Brand & Messaging Guide

**Version 1.0 · June 2026 · CC0, like everything we make**

---

## The core idea

Every cybersecurity brand on earth looks the same: black background, neon green, padlocks, hooded figures, fear. Grass Roots Cyber refuses all of it. We are not a fortress. We are a garden.

The brand concept is **the community garden for digital life**. Security isn't a product you buy or a wall you hide behind — it's a practice you tend, in public, with your neighbors. Gardens are the perfect metaphor because they're already how this organization thinks: bottom-up, regenerative, seasonal, shared, and a little messy. The charter wrote the brand before the brand existed ("gremlins of resilience, not gatekeepers of compliance"). This guide just gives it a coat of paint.

**Positioning statement:** Grass Roots Cyber is the Central Illinois community where regular people learn to protect their digital lives the same way they'd tend a garden — together, in the open, with free tools, and without fear-mongering.

**One-line answer to "what is this?":** "We're a neighborhood cybersecurity club. Free, open source, no sales pitch — bring your laptop."

## Voice

The voice is **a knowledgeable neighbor leaning over the fence** — warm, practical, plainspoken, with a streak of mischief. Three rules:

1. **Plain verbs, no fear.** We never sell with dread. Not "Hackers are coming for your family" but "Updating your router takes ten minutes. Bring it Tuesday, we'll do it together." Fear-based security marketing is the thing we exist to replace.
2. **Garden words over war words.** We patch, tend, grow, plant, weed, and harvest. We don't battle, defend, fortify, or wage. The one exception: "Patch Party" keeps "patch" because it's both gardening and software, which is the whole brand in one word.
3. **Mischief is allowed.** The gremlin energy from the charter is real and should show up — in event names, in 404 pages, in stickers. It never shows up in safety-critical instructions, which are always boring and precise on purpose.

Words we use: neighbors, tinkerers, plots, seeds, patches, the commons, mutual aid, field guide, locally grown.
Words we never use: solutions, leverage, synergy, cyber threat landscape, hardening (say "tuning up"), end users (say "people").

## Visual identity

### Palette — "Seed Packet"

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Soil | `#2B2118` | Ink, footer, dark surfaces |
| Field Paper | `#EFEFE2` | Background — sun-faded, green-tinted paper, not cream |
| Leaf | `#3E7C3F` | Primary brand green, headings, buttons |
| Sprout | `#7FB069` | Accents, hover states, growth moments |
| Marigold | `#E0A458` | Call-to-action & warning accent |
| Terminal | `#9FE870` | Code, mono text on Soil only |

Marigold is the one warm color, and it has a story you should tell out loud: gardeners plant marigolds among vegetables because they repel pests. **Marigold is the firewall of the garden.** Anything marked marigold is a protective action.

The "hacker green on black" look is allowed in exactly one place: inside terminal/code blocks, framed by the garden around them. The terminal lives in our garden — not the other way around.

### Typography

- **Display: Bricolage Grotesque** — sturdy, characterful, slightly off-kilter, free. Use heavy weights, tight, for headlines only.
- **Body: Atkinson Hyperlegible** — designed by the Braille Institute for maximum legibility, free. Choosing it is itself a brand statement: accessibility is mutual aid.
- **Utility: IBM Plex Mono** — code, data, labels, dates, the signature wall.

### Motifs

- **The seed packet:** dashed "cut here" borders, specimen labels ("GRC-001 · RESILIENCE, COMMON"), planting instructions as numbered steps. Use for membership cards, event flyers, and the join flow.
- **The plot grid:** the membership roll rendered as a community garden — every signer gets a plot. Always show one open plot labeled "This plot is open."
- **Patch the Gremlin:** the mascot. A small, friendly gremlin holding a watering can in one hand and a USB drive in the other. Appears on stickers, slides, and the 404 page. Never in formal documents.

## Messaging pillars

1. **Locally grown.** We meet in person, in Central Illinois, at libraries and farmers markets. Security advice from someone you'll see again hits different than advice from a vendor.
2. **Open by default.** Our charter, finances, meeting notes, and even our website source are public and CC0. We run the org in a public Git repo. Fork us.
3. **Mutual aid threat model.** Nobody secures themselves alone. We protect each other — the retiree's email, the small shop's point-of-sale, the kid's first Linux install.
4. **Playful practice.** People learn what they enjoy. Tabletop games, patch parties, and gremlin humor are pedagogy, not decoration.

## Program names (the product line)

- **Patch Parties** — monthly bring-your-device tune-up meetups. Flagship.
- **The Seed Vault** — our open knowledge base plus physical offline USB knowledge kits.
- **Gremlin Drills** — lightweight tabletop incident exercises for families, clubs, and small shops.
- **Field Notes** — the dispatch, published to the open web, Nostr, and the Fediverse.
